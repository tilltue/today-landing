There is a word hiding inside this app: print. The moment you save a day's entry, the photo you just chose appears briefly as a single polaroid card with a white border. The home-screen widget is named "Today's Print," too. At first it looks like a bit of decoration, but it is actually the most deliberate part of the app.

## The weight of a single print

A photo in your camera roll and a printed photo on your desk can be the same photo and still carry different weight. The thousands in the roll are part of a stream that passes with one swipe; the one on paper is an object at rest. Someone chose it, decided to keep it, and that is why it is there.

We did not want the one photo you choose each day to feel like just another image in that stream. So at the moment of saving, the app shows today's photo as a single print — a sign that this is not one of thousands flowing by, but the one you decided to keep.

## Filters as the temperature of memory

The app ships with five filters borrowed from film: warm tone, soft grain, vintage film, classic slide, toy camera. The original, untouched, works too.

![The same photo with each of the five filters — original, warm tone, soft grain, vintage film, classic slide, toy camera](/assets/images/stories/paper-and-film-filters.jpg)

These filters have a different goal from correction. They do not smooth skin or brighten colors. They add grain, fade the palette a little, darken the corners. They move a photo from "an image taken just now" toward the tone of "a memory that has aged a bit."

Memory is not stored at original quality, after all. It stays a little warmer, a little faded. A filter simply applies that temperature in advance.

## One frame, chosen like film

If you have ever shot with a film camera, you know the feeling. With only twenty-four frames, you look once more before pressing the shutter. Ironically, that constraint makes the photo stay with you longer — because the scene was chosen with care.

The one-photo-a-day rule borrows that instinct. In an era of infinite shots and infinite uploads, if your day's roll held a single frame, which scene would you keep? Getting to ask that question once every evening is everything this app wanted to take from film.

## A print on your home screen

Somewhere along the way, photos stopped being things we touch. There is a moment of taking, but no moment of holding.

That is why the home-screen widget is named "Today's Print." Leave today's entry, and that one photo settles onto your home screen. On days you have not written yet, an empty print waits there instead, inviting you to capture today. Meeting one piece of paper for the day on the home screen in your pocket — we believe it is small sensations like this, more than grand features, that keep a record going.
