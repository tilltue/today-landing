title: A diary you're allowed to skip
summary: Why a broken streak makes people delete the app — and what a journal looks like without one.

There's a familiar sequence. You write for several days running. Then one evening gets away from you. You open the app the next morning and the number that had been climbing is back to zero. Something deflates a little, and after that the icon sits on your home screen untouched.

## Why a broken streak ends the habit

Streaks were built to help. Attaching a small, visible win to something hard to start is a reasonable idea, and for the first few weeks it genuinely works.

The trouble starts when the mechanism reverses. The larger the number grows, the more there is to lose. To someone forty-seven days in, a single missed evening doesn't feel like one blank day — it feels like forty-seven days destroyed. And once it's gone, starting over from zero is harder than starting was in the first place.

At that point the app quietly changes jobs. What was a tool for remembering becomes a report card, and opening it becomes an act of confirming you failed. So people delete it. Not because they stopped wanting to write, but because they'd rather not see a screen that tells them they're behind.

## Guilt doesn't produce more entries

Worth separating two things that get conflated: the goal isn't to write every day. The goal is to have something to look back on.

Aim for daily and a missed day becomes a failure. Aim for *kept* and a missed day is just a day you didn't write. Open that calendar three years later and the gaps take nothing away from what's there. Sometimes they add something — a sparse stretch is an accurate record of a month that was genuinely too full.

## What it looks like to allow gaps

So there's no streak counter in this app. Nothing counts how many days you've missed, and nothing notifies you that it's been a while. An empty square on the calendar is just an empty square. It isn't marked in red, and it doesn't tell you what you skipped.

You can also write to past dates. If you're going through yesterday's photos this afternoon and want to keep one, keep it. Catching up isn't a misuse of the app — plenty of people sit down once on the weekend, scroll back through the week, and fill in what mattered. That's a perfectly good way to use it.

## Coming back

What makes a record accumulate isn't going unbroken. It's **being easy to return to after it breaks**.

If you can open the app after two empty months and pick today's photo without being reminded of the two months, the record continues. If you're shown the gap first, it usually ends there. That's a difference in design, not in willpower.

So skip a few days. Skip a season. On the day you feel like opening it again, choose one photo from that day.
