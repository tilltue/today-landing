title: Where does your diary actually live?
summary: What happens when you delete the app, clean out your camera roll, or switch phones.

---

Read enough reviews of journaling apps and a pattern shows up. The one-star reviews are rarely about missing features. They're about **losing what was written**.

"Three years of entries, gone." "Switched phones and couldn't get any of it back." Those reviews read differently from the rest. An awkward interface is something you tolerate. A lost year isn't recoverable.

So before we added features, we wanted a clear answer to one question: **where does this diary actually live?**

## Apps that point at your photos vs. apps that hold them

Start with the photos, because there's a distinction here that's invisible until it costs you.

Many apps, when you attach a photo to an entry, only store **a reference to that photo in your camera roll**. Inside the app everything looks fine. But months later you clear out your camera roll to free up space, and the photos in your entries go blank along with it. You were tidying up storage; you didn't know you were deleting your journal.

This app copies the photo into its own storage the moment you choose it. Delete the original from your camera roll later and the entry keeps its picture. It costs a little more space on your device. What it buys is that your record doesn't depend on you never cleaning up your photos.

## Why no account is the safer answer, not the riskier one

There's no signup here and no login. You open the app and start.

"No account" tends to raise a worry, though: if I'm not signed in anywhere, is anything actually being kept?

It's the other way around. No account means **your entries are never uploaded to our servers**. They're written to your device first, then backed up into your own iCloud. Both of those are your space, not ours. We can't read what's in them, and we don't need to.

A diary holds the things you don't put anywhere else. Keeping that off of someone else's servers seemed worth giving up some convenience for.

## Switching phones, and deleting the app

This is usually where the real anxiety sits.

If entries only lived on the device, a new phone would be the end of them. So a backup copy goes into your iCloud as well: the photo, the text, and the date, kept together as one bundle per entry. Open the app for the first time on a new phone and it reads those bundles back in. The same thing happens if you delete the app by accident and reinstall it.

Devices signed into the same iCloud account pass entries back and forth through those bundles. There's no sync button to remember and nothing to log into.

## A thing meant to be kept

Journaling apps carry a requirement most apps don't: **they only mean anything if they're still around later**. A photo editor just has to work today. A diary has to still be there in three years, on a phone you haven't bought yet.

That's why this part came before the features. Photos held as copies, entries stored in your own space, and a record that comes back on a new device. It's the least visible part of the app, and the part that matters most the longer you use it.
